The Opportunity

Why Pre-IPO, And Why Now

Companies now stay private for a decade or more. The part of their growth that used to belong to the public market happens before the ticker exists.

Pre-IPO Access, Before The Public

You buy at the valuation of the round we entered, in companies that have not listed and may not list for years. By the time a ticker exists, that price is history.

Institutional Valuations For Individuals

You come in on the same round, at the same price, on the same terms as the institution sitting next to us in the block. Not a feeder fund of a fund, not a synthetic note.

Exclusive Deal Flow

Late-stage rounds are allocated privately, in seven-figure blocks, to firms already on the cap table. Most investors never see these names offered at all — there is no listing to browse.

Capital Deployed Quickly

When a round opens, it closes in days. Because Lumara has already taken the block, your allocation goes to work as soon as documents and the wire are in — no waiting for a raise.

Exit At The Listing

Positions are held through to a liquidity event — an IPO or an acquisition — typically within an 18 to 36 month window from our entry. Secondary sales can sometimes open earlier, though none is promised.

What The Public Market Can’t Offer

A public share is priced by the tape every afternoon. A private position is priced by the next round. It is a different asset class — with a different risk, too: no liquidity, and the real possibility of total loss.

$25,000, Not $1,000,000

Because we take the whole block first, a $25,000 position becomes possible in rounds that are otherwise sold in seven-figure pieces.

Our Money In First

Lumara’s own capital goes into every position before a single investor dollar. We do not offer allocations we would not hold ourselves.

Questions

Common Questions

What exactly do you do?+
Lumara Investment Partners has 25 years in the private market, $140M under management and 1,200 investors. We take institutional allocations in late-stage private companies such as OpenAI, Anthropic, Stripe and Databricks, and split those positions into $25,000 pieces for accredited investors. Same round, same price, same terms as our own capital.
How does this work?+
A fifteen-minute consultation verifies your accreditation and walks you through the memo on what we currently have room in. If you proceed, you e-sign, wire your allocation, and hold a piece of the block we took. The position exits at listing or acquisition.
Why hasn’t my advisor shown me this?+
Because the plumbing does not reach them. Late-stage rounds are allocated in seven-figure blocks to funds and family offices. Unless a firm splits its own institutional position, there is nothing for an advisor to offer at $25,000.
Why is it limited to a few investors a month?+
Two reasons, both practical. A block is a fixed size, so the number of $25,000 pieces in it is finite. And every investor is walked through the memo personally and verified properly, which is not something we can do at volume. When a month’s allocations are taken, the next opening is the next round we take.
What are realistic expectations?+
We do not quote projected returns. What we can tell you: 9 out of 10 of the companies we have ever invested in have gone public, and positions are typically positioned to list within 18 to 36 months of our entry. That window is based on where a company sits in its life cycle, not a date anyone can promise.
How do I get my money out?+
You don’t, until the company lists or is acquired. These positions do not trade. This is illiquid capital and should never be money you need in the next four years. That is the honest cost of buying at the private price.
Is an IPO guaranteed?+
No. No company is obligated to list, and a position can lose value or fail entirely. What we offer is 25 years of judgment about which companies are positioned to list, and a record you can check.
Am I actually eligible?+
If you’re an accredited investor, yes. Because this offer is made publicly, the law requires us to verify that properly - brokerage statements, a CPA or attorney letter, or a third-party verification service - rather than a checkbox. Most investors clear it in a day.
What does it cost?+
The minimum position is $25,000. The fee structure is set out in the subscription documents and walked through, in writing, on the consultation before you commit to anything.
Which companies do you have room in right now?+
That changes, which is why it lives on the consultation and not on this page. The current memo covers each open name: what it does, the round, and the terms.
