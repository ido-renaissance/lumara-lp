The Process

How It Works

Three phases. The first costs you fifteen minutes and commits you to nothing.

A late-stage private round is sold in single blocks of one million dollars and up. Nothing in that process accepts $25,000, which is why you have never been offered one. Lumara buys the block, puts its own capital in first, then splits the position among its investors. Fifteen minutes on the phone confirms you’re accredited and walks you through the memo on what we currently have room in.

PHASE I

The Call

Fifteen minutes. We verify you’re accredited properly, not with a checkbox, and take you through the One-Pager on each company we currently have room in: what it does, the round we bought, and the terms you would hold.

What we need from youFifteen minutes. Proof of accreditation (statement, letter, or third-party verification).
You know exactly which private companies you could hold, at what price, before deciding anything.
PHASE II

The Allocation Split

Lumara has already bought the institutional block with its own capital in first. Your $25,000 or more takes a piece of that same position. Same round, same price, same terms as the firm sitting next to you.

What we need from youE-sign the subscription documents. Wire your allocation.
You hold the position directly through the block we bought, not a note that tracks it.
PHASE III

The Window

The companies we buy are typically positioned to list within 18 to 36 months of our entry. You receive the firm’s updates while the position is private, and the exit comes at listing or acquisition.

What we need from youNothing. Patience is the asset.
When the ticker finally appears, you are reading the news as an owner instead of a buyer.

Questions

Common Questions

What exactly do you do?+
Lumara is a private-markets fund with 25 years of history, $140M under management and 1,200 investors. We buy institutional allocations in late-stage private companies such as OpenAI, Anthropic, Stripe and Databricks, and split those positions into $25,000 pieces for accredited investors. Same round, same price, same terms as our own capital.
How does this work?+
A fifteen-minute call verifies your accreditation and walks you through the memo on what we currently have room in. If you proceed, you e-sign, wire your allocation, and hold a piece of the block we bought. The position exits at listing or acquisition.
Why hasn’t my advisor shown me this?+
Because the plumbing does not reach them. Late-stage rounds are allocated in seven-figure blocks to funds and family offices. Unless a firm splits its own institutional position, there is nothing for an advisor to offer at $25,000.
What are realistic expectations?+
We do not quote projected returns. What we can tell you: 92% of the companies we have ever invested in have gone public, and positions are typically positioned to list within 18 to 36 months of our entry. That window is based on where a company sits in its life cycle, not a date anyone can promise.
How do I get my money out?+
You don’t, until the company lists or is acquired. These positions do not trade. This is illiquid capital and should never be money you need in the next four years. That is the honest cost of buying at the private price.
Is an IPO guaranteed?+
No. No company is obligated to list, and a position can lose value or fail entirely. What we offer is 25 years of judgment about which companies are positioned to list, and a record you can check.
Am I actually eligible?+
If you’re an accredited investor, yes. Because this offer is made publicly, the law requires us to verify that properly - brokerage statements, a CPA or attorney letter, or a third-party verification service - rather than a checkbox. Most investors clear it in a day.
What does it cost?+
The minimum position is $25,000. The fee structure is set out in the subscription documents and walked through, in writing, on the call before you commit to anything.
Which companies do you have room in right now?+
That changes, which is why it lives on the call and not on this page. The current memo covers each open name: what it does, the round, and the terms.
